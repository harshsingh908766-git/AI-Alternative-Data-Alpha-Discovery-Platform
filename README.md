01. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

The AI + Alternative Data Alpha Discovery Platform (AADA) is a comprehensive research architecture designed to investigate whether heterogeneous alternative datasets, when integrated with artificial intelligence, financial econometrics, statistical learning, causal inference and portfolio intelligence, can produce economically meaningful and statistically robust investment signals.

The platform addresses a fundamental challenge in systematic investing:

«How can fragmented, heterogeneous and high-dimensional information be transformed into reproducible, explainable, statistically validated and economically implementable investment intelligence?»

Rather than treating machine learning predictions as investment alpha, the architecture establishes a complete research lifecycle:

Discover → Acquire → Validate → Transform → Engineer → Model → Test → Falsify → Optimise → Govern → Monitor

Its central objective is not to maximise backtested returns or produce impressive predictive metrics.

Its objective is to establish a rigorous framework through which investment hypotheses can be generated, tested, challenged, rejected or potentially operationalised.

02. THE RESEARCH THESIS

The platform is built around three interconnected research pillars.

Pillar I — Information Hypothesis

Alternative datasets may contain incremental information that is not fully captured by conventional financial and fundamental datasets.

Pillar II — Transformation Hypothesis

AI, machine learning, econometrics, NLP, graph analytics and statistical methods may transform heterogeneous information into measurable predictive signals.

Pillar III — Governance Hypothesis

Data provenance, point-in-time integrity, reproducibility, model validation and risk governance are necessary conditions for credible quantitative research.

Central Research Proposition

«An appropriately governed AI + Alternative Data Alpha Discovery Platform may improve the systematic identification and validation of investment signals by integrating heterogeneous alternative datasets with rigorous statistical, econometric and machine-learning methods; however, predictive association does not by itself establish economic alpha, causal effectiveness, investability or persistence.»

This proposition is deliberately conditional and empirically falsifiable.

07. CAUSAL INTELLIGENCE

Predictive association does not establish causality.

The platform therefore incorporates a separate causal investigation framework.

Research methodologies:

1. Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)
2. Difference-in-Differences
3. Instrumental Variables
4. Natural Experiments
5. Event Studies
6. Causal Forests
7. Heterogeneous Treatment Effects
8. Sensitivity Analysis

Causal identification requires defensible assumptions, valid instruments or suitable experimental conditions.

Where these conditions are unavailable, results must remain predictive rather than causal.

17. RESEARCH QUALITY & VALIDATION STANDARDS

The platform adopts a research-first evaluation protocol.

Data integrity

- Verify source licensing and permitted usage.
- Preserve immutable raw data.
- Maintain data provenance.
- Record ingestion and availability timestamps.
- Test temporal alignment.
- Detect missingness and revisions.
- Document survivorship risk.

Statistical integrity

- Define hypotheses before testing.
- Specify baseline models.
- Correct for multiple testing.
- Report null findings.
- Separate exploratory and confirmatory research.
- Use strictly temporal out-of-sample evaluation.

Backtesting integrity

- Prevent look-ahead bias.
- Prevent survivorship bias.
- Account for corporate actions.
- Model transaction costs.
- Evaluate slippage and market impact.
- Assess capacity.
- Perform regime and sensitivity testing.

Reproducibility

- Pin dependencies.
- Version datasets and features.
- Record random seeds.
- Store experiment configurations.
- Preserve model artefacts.
- Retain negative results.

19. LIMITATIONS & RESEARCH TRANSPARENCY

This project explicitly acknowledges the following limitations:

1. Architectural design alone cannot establish empirical validity.
2. Actual alternative-data access may be restricted by cost, licensing or privacy.
3. Historical alternative datasets may have limited temporal depth.
4. ML models may overfit and fail to generalise.
5. Multiple-testing corrections may not eliminate all data-snooping risk.
6. Causal identification requires appropriate identification conditions.
7. Transaction costs depend on strategy, capital, liquidity and market conditions.
8. Regime changes can invalidate historical relationships.
9. Portfolio capacity cannot be inferred from predictive accuracy alone.
10. Research results require independent replication.

No hypothetical or illustrative performance metric should be represented as actual investment performance.
